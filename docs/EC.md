Edward (Zhixing) Cao is an Assistant Professor at Queen’s University, Canada. His research develops mathematical, computational, and AI-assisted methods for understanding stochastic biochemical reaction systems, with a particular focus on gene-expression dynamics at the single-cell level.

One major direction of his work is stochastic gene-expression modeling. His group studies how transcriptional bursting, promoter switching, feedback regulation, cell-cycle effects, mRNA maturation, and nuclear-cytoplasmic transport shape the distributions of mRNA and protein copy numbers. Rather than relying only on simulation, the group derives analytical probability distributions and probability-generating-function solutions that make these stochastic mechanisms interpretable and testable.

Another central theme is parameter inference from noisy biological data. The group develops methods for fitting mechanistic stochastic models to single-cell, population-snapshot, and spatial transcriptomic measurements. These methods are designed to quantify kinetic parameters, evaluate parameter identifiability, distinguish competing transcriptional mechanisms, and link cell-to-cell variability with underlying biochemical regulation.

His group also works on scalable computation for chemical master equations and non-Markovian biochemical kinetics. By combining stochastic-process theory, generating-function analysis, neural-network approximations, and reduced numerical solvers, the group builds tools for reaction networks with delays, hidden intermediate steps, and high-dimensional state spaces. These approaches aim to preserve mechanistic interpretability while making parameter inference and distribution prediction computationally practical.

More broadly, his research connects control theory, stochastic modeling, machine learning, and systems biology. Representative work has appeared in *Nature Communications*, *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, and *Physical Review Letters*.
